Dear Editors,

I am submitting "Sampling design bounds parameter recovery in a reduced CT contrast-kinetics model, and a closed-form fit nearly attains the bound" for consideration as Original Research in Computer
Methods and Programs in Biomedicine.

Physics-informed and amortized neural estimators are now routinely applied to
contrast-kinetics and perfusion models. That work is validated where dynamic imaging is
dense: myocardial perfusion MRI, DCE-MRI and CT perfusion sample tens of time points. A
routine contrast-enhanced CT acquires two to four. This paper asks what such an
acquisition determines about physiology, and whether that limit predicts the error
estimators actually make.

It reports four things. First, the reduced Bae compartment model has an exact
one-parameter scale symmetry: three volumes, the cardiac output and the attenuation
constant scale together with no effect on any enhancement curve, confirmed to machine
precision in the forward simulation. Those five quantities are therefore not separately
recoverable from enhancement at any sampling density — a property of the model rather
than a shortage of data, and one that any fit reporting them is implicitly assuming away.

Second, with the necessary fixing made explicit, the Cramér–Rao bound on the remaining
parameters is 70% relative for a pre-contrast plus portal venous study, 14% when an
arterial phase is added, and 13% with a fourth. A pre-contrast acquisition carries
sensitivity identically zero, so a two-phase study is one informative measurement; taken
alone the arterial phase carries 3.5 times the sensitivity of the portal venous phase and
23 times the delayed one.

Third — and this was the prespecified endpoint, chosen because it could fail — the bound
predicts measured recovery error across eight sampling designs, each repeated over twenty
independent noise draws, with Spearman correlations of 0.95 to 0.98 (p ≤ 0.0003) and the
same for all three estimators.

Fourth, the closed-form least-squares fit comes within 7% of that bound, at a median
1.07 times it. The physics-informed hybrid runs at 1.52 and amortized inference at 1.72.
What limits recovery here is the acquisition, not the estimator, and the classical
estimator already extracts most of what the acquisition contains.

The same limit is visible in the real data without any Fisher calculation. Thirteen of
the twenty public multi-phase studies are two-phase, and a two-phase study carries one
informative measurement because a pre-contrast acquisition has no contrast in it. On
every one of those the closed form interpolates the datum exactly, at machine precision,
which is the signature of an underdetermined fit rather than of accuracy. On the seven
studies with three or more phases the closed form and the physics-informed hybrid are
indistinguishable, both at 0.127 curve NRMSE. The closed form's apparent three-fold
advantage across all twenty was thirteen zeros pulling down a mean.

I want to be explicit about what is not claimed. Applying physics-informed networks to
contrast kinetics is prior art and is cited as such; Fisher-information-based experimental
design is textbook and is cited as such. The contribution is the identifiability of this
model at these phase counts, the test that the bound predicts error, and the finding that
the classical estimator saturates it. The result on real multi-phase CT concerns curve
reconstruction only, because there is no ground-truth physiology there.

Two earlier claims from an internal draft of this work were withdrawn during preparation
and are worth mentioning because they shaped the method reported here. A parameter-error
advantage for the physics-informed estimator turned out to rest on a single noise draw
per condition, and reverses over twenty. An advantage for amortized inference on curve
error turned out to come from an undertrained network that returned a constant regardless
of its input; retrained to a budget at which it demonstrably uses its input — correlation
0.974 with held-out cardiac output — it no longer shows one. The manuscript reports the
retrained network and the realisation count, and the calibration is in the frozen results
so a reader can check which was done.

I chose this journal because reporting new computational methodology alongside
demonstrable software is what it states it exists for, and because that is the shape of
this work rather than something it must apologise for. The software is installable, tested
and deterministic; every number in the manuscript is emitted by executed code into
machine-readable files and resolved into the text at build time, and the test suite fails
if the two disagree.
